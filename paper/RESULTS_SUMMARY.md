## 6. Findings (plain statements)

1. **English injections are much more effective than every Bangla form, in all five models.** `en` ASR 34.0–56.8% vs 2.0–20.7% for `bn_mt`, `bn_llm` and `banglish_llm`. C3 pooled: 45.4% → 7.9%, Δ -37.5 pp [-46.4, -28.8]; glmer OR 0.022, p_Holm <1e-4. Every per-model C3 test is Holm-significant (5/5). In this setting, writing the injection in Bangla made it *less* effective, not more.
2. **C1 is null: LLM-written Bangla did not beat machine-translated Bangla.** Pooled Δ +1.0 pp [-2.1, 4.0], 90% CI [-1.6, 3.5] lies inside ±5 pp (TOST equivalent: yes); glmer OR 1.298 [0.694, 2.427], p_Holm 0.350. 0/5 models Holm-significant. The only nominal per-model effect is Gemma (Δ +4.7 pp, p 0.039, p_Holm 0.195), the self-generated cell. Per-model TOST shows equivalence within ±5 pp only for Granite, Qwen; for the others n = 150 cannot exclude a ±5 pp difference.
3. **Romanised Banglish (`banglish_llm`) was at least as effective as both Bengali-script conditions in 4 of 5 models** (gpt-oss, Ministral, Qwen, Gemma; exploratory). BH-significant non-English pairs: Gemma † banglish_llm − bn_mt +6.7 pp; gpt-oss banglish_llm − bn_mt +7.3 pp; gpt-oss banglish_llm − bn_llm +6.0 pp; Qwen banglish_llm − bn_mt +10.0 pp; Qwen banglish_llm − bn_llm +10.7 pp. Granite goes the other way (not significant).
4. **Model ranking flips with language.** Granite is the most hijackable model in English (56.8%) and the least in Bangla (2.0–4.7%); Qwen is the least hijackable in English (34.0%) and the most in Bangla (10.0–20.7%). A plausible reading — not tested here (no comprehension probe was run) — is that the injection only works if the model understands it, so stronger multilingual models are *more* exposed to Bangla injections.
5. **Utility under attack is higher under every Bangla injection than under English ones, in every model** (e.g. `en` vs `bn_mt`: gpt-oss 40.0 vs 53.3, Ministral 45.6 vs 54.0, Granite 34.5 vs 71.6, Qwen 76.0 vs 82.7, Gemma 70.0 vs 77.3%), consistent with fewer successful hijacks derailing the user task.
6. **Agents almost never answered or acted in Bengali.** Episodes whose final output or tool arguments are mostly Bengali: max 0.0% / 0.0% per cell (`paper/results/fasttrack_v1_20261007/primary/T10c_output_language.csv`); only 15 of 2991 attacked episodes exceed the §4.4 20% Bengali-share threshold (14 of them L2 failures, which that branch of the gate selects); so at least 162 of the 176 gated episodes are gated because they touch an attacker target (`paper/results/fasttrack_v1_20261007/primary/episodes_tidy.csv`).
7. **Normalisation (L2 vs raw L1) does not create the gap.** L2 − L1 per cell: non-English -2.00 to 0.67 pp; English -0.67 to 5.41 pp (`paper/results/fasttrack_v1_20261007/primary/T4_undercount.csv`), i.e. normalisation mostly raises English ASR. The low Bangla ASR is not a scoring artefact of Bangla-language outputs: identifier-only goals (scoring group A, where the check is an exact recipient/IBAN/URL) show the same gap (`paper/results/fasttrack_v1_20261007/primary/T12_asr_by_scoring_group.csv`).
8. **Suite reweighting** (§10.4) lowers every ASR but `en` stays above every Bangla form in every model (`paper/results/fasttrack_v1_20261007/primary/T5_suite_reweighted.csv`): across models, `en` reweighted 25.8–53.6% vs `bn_llm` 3.5–7.1%.

## 7. Surprise (POST HOC, exploratory — D40): Bengali script often never reached the agent as script

AgentDojo 0.1.35 formats structured tool results with `yaml.safe_dump` (PyYAML default `allow_unicode=False`), so Bengali characters inside structured outputs reach the agent as `\uXXXX` escapes; plain-string outputs keep the script. `en`/`banglish_llm` are ASCII and unaffected. Rendering per episode: `paper/results/fasttrack_v1_20261007/primary/T11_injection_rendering.csv`; ASR by stratum: `paper/results/fasttrack_v1_20261007/primary/T11b_asr_by_rendering_stratum.csv`; contrasts by stratum: `paper/results/fasttrack_v1_20261007/primary/T11c_contrasts_by_rendering_stratum.csv`.

* Per model, 81–99 of 150 `bn_mt` episodes were escaped and 46–52 kept the script (the rest never saw the injection).
* Escaped stratum: `bn_mt`/`bn_llm` ASR = 0.0% in every model (pooled primary pool n = 359: `en` 36.5%, `banglish_llm` 3.3%).
* Raw-script stratum (pooled primary pool, n = 196): `en` 71.3% [57.1, 84.0], `bn_mt` 21.4% [11.1, 34.1], `bn_llm` 24.5% [12.5, 39.0], `banglish_llm` 30.1% [18.6, 43.2].
* So even where the agent saw real Bengali script, English was far stronger (`bn_llm − en` -47.2 pp [-59.0, -34.5]) and C1 stays null (`bn_llm − bn_mt` +3.1 pp [-5.6, 13.2]).
* The strata are not randomised: they follow suite/tool type (all of travel is raw script, all of workspace escaped; banking and slack are mixed), and `en` is also weaker in the escaped stratum. Treat these numbers as descriptive. The headline `bn_*` ASR therefore mixes two stimuli (script and `\u` escapes); the paper must say so.

## 11. Caveats

* **n = 150 pairs per cell** (35 injection-task clusters). Per-cell 95% CI half-widths range 3.0–14.2 pp (median 7.3); equivalence within ±5 pp can rarely be shown per model.
* **4-bit quantised open models** on llama.cpp, temperature 0, one run per episode (no repeat/flip-rate data). Results may not transfer to full-precision or hosted frontier models.
* **Error handling** (D38.2): 2 model-output errors counted as failures, 1 timeout missing, Granite 2 pairs excluded for context overflow; both alternatives change no conclusion (§8).
* **Self-generated payloads** (D24.2/D38.3): Gemma wrote the LLM payloads and the second-generator (Qwen) subset was not run, so Gemma's `bn_llm`/`banglish_llm` cells are flagged and kept out of pooled primary inference.
* **Qwen pilot vs main setting (D31):** the Qwen pilot ran with thinking ON (template default); the main grid ran thinking OFF (plan §11.3). Qwen main-grid numbers are not comparable to its pilot.
* **Pilots used 4 parallel slots vs 1 in the main grid (D32);** pilot throughput/utility are context only, never pooled with the main grid.
* **Granite exclusions:** n = 148 per attacked condition, 96 no-attack tasks.
* **Rendering artefact (D40, post hoc):** in 54–67% of `bn_*` episodes per model the agent saw `\u` escapes, not Bengali script; stratified numbers are exploratory and confounded with suite. The post hoc Unicode-preserving rerun (§7b, D41) is the robustness check.
* **Scope:** one benchmark (AgentDojo v1.2.2), 150 of its pairs, no defences, single injection wrapper (W1).
* **Judge:** all headline ASR here is deterministic (L2); semantic successes the checks miss enter only the judge-augmented numbers in §10, which rest on two open LLM judges without a human audit.
